Evidence and sources

Sources were last checked on 6 November 2026. They motivate the design. None of them supplies patient-level data to the synthetic cohort.

Clinical sources

  • S1. USPSTF (2021). Colorectal Cancer: Screening. Supports the average-risk, asymptomatic screening context and ages 45 to 75. It does not prescribe our 14-, 30- or 90-day workflow thresholds.
  • S2. JAMA Network Open (2023). Rates of Follow-up Colonoscopy After a Positive Stool-Based Screening Test Result for Colorectal Cancer Among Health Care Organizations in the US, 2017-2020. Motivation for an incomplete follow-up scenario. This is a mixed stool-test cohort, not a FIT-only estimate for all settings.
  • S3. Corley et al., JAMA (2017). Association Between Time to Colonoscopy After a Positive Fecal Test Result and Risk of Colorectal Cancer and Cancer Stage at Diagnosis. DOI: 10.1001/jama.2017.3634. Observational association; not proof that delay causes cancer.
  • S4. JAMA Network Open (2024). Development of a Follow-Up Measure to Ensure Complete Screening for Colorectal Cancer. Supports feasibility of a defined follow-up quality measure. Does not validate this implementation.
  • S5. NCQA (2026). HEDIS MY 2027: What's New, What's Changed, What's Retired. Announces COF-E. Our indicator is not certified or presented as an exact reproduction of NCQA specifications. The traditional screening-coverage measure and follow-up completion are distinct.

Public map data

  • S6. CDC dataset. PLACES county data, 2025 release. Dataset ID swc5-untb; COLON_SCREEN; state IA. Raw retrieval includes 198 rows (99 crude and 99 age-adjusted estimates). Only CrdPrv is used in the map. Source query: https://data.cdc.gov/resource/swc5-untb.json?%24limit=500&stateabbr=IA&measureid=COLON_SCREEN . The checksum of the raw download is kept in data/manifest.json.
  • S7. CDC definition. PLACES prevention measures. The measure is up-to-date colorectal screening coverage among ages 45 to 75, estimated from population surveys and demographic data. It is not a FIT-positive clinical care cascade.
  • S8. CDC release notes. Current Release Notes. The 2025 release carries forward 2022 BRFSS colorectal-screening data. The interface shows both "2025 release" and "2022 source year" so the publication date is not mistaken for the observation year.
  • S9. Geometry. Plotly county GeoJSON, also used in Plotly's county choropleth documentation. The downloaded US county dataset was filtered by state FIPS prefix 19. This is illustrative Census-derived geometry, not a current legal-boundary representation. No provider tiles or runtime map service are used.

Simulation assumptions

The 6.8% positive-result input, return/referral/booking probabilities, demographic mix, diagnostic findings, barriers, calendar delays and workflow thresholds are simulation choices made for this project. They are not estimates of prevalence or diagnostic accuracy, and results from the simulation are not evidence of any improvement caused by Loop.

Modeling methods

  • statsmodels SurvfuncRight: right-censored survival estimation. Version used: statsmodels 0.14.5.
  • scikit-learn LogisticRegression: regularized binary classifier. Version used: scikit-learn 1.7.2.
  • scikit-learn data leakage guidance: training/evaluation separation.
  • scikit-learn probability calibration: reliability diagrams and probability scoring.

These references describe methods. They are not evidence that the model works on real patients. All evaluation results are reported, including the rule baseline that outperformed the model.

Navigation evidence and modeling references

  • S10. Coronado et al., Annals of Internal Medicine (2025). Patient Navigation to Improve Colonoscopy Completion After an Abnormal Stool Test Result: A Randomized Controlled Trial. DOI 10.7326/ANNALS-24-01885. One-year completion was 55.1% with navigation versus 42.1% with usual care (13.0 percentage points; 95% CI 6.5 to 19.4). Baseline predicted completion probability did not moderate the intervention effect. We take this as support for testing navigation. It does not show that higher-risk patients benefit more, and its one-year effect is not used to set the 180-day scenario.
  • S11. Ganguly et al., Journal of General Internal Medicine (2026). Patient Navigation to Improve Colonoscopy Completion Following an Abnormal Stool-test Result. DOI 10.1007/s11606-025-10151-2. The positive-FIT subgroup was small (89 participants); navigation included scheduling, preparation and social support. We used it to choose task types. It does not calibrate the simulator.
  • scikit-learn ensemble methods and permutation feature importance. Fixed random-forest parameters are specified in the simulation protocol. Permutation importance on the validation set is descriptive only and was not used to select features.

The base 85% task advancement, dedicated weekly capacity, task costs, missing-record probabilities, and latent needs are uncalibrated assumptions. Simulated improvements are not expected clinical effects. The simulator uses its own discrete-event queue and does not depend on SimPy.

Audit method

  • Evaluation of clinical prediction models (part 2): how to undertake an external validation study, BMJ 2024. Supports separately assessing discrimination, calibration and decision usefulness. The current experiment audits a simulated change of mechanism and a change of label. It is not an external clinical validation and does not claim TRIPOD compliance.

Natural recovery, outside appointment failure and revised navigation response are prespecified simulation assumptions. No new clinical data were used to estimate them. The audit looks separately at what the model is for, which labels a clinic could obtain, and how policies behave when patients share resources.
